# Model Card: Materials Data Lab (v1.5.0)

## Model Details
- **Architecture**: XGBoost Regressor (Tuned)
- **Version**: 1.5.0
- **Input Features**: 12 compositional variables (wt%) + `temper_temp_c` + `log_time`.
- **Output**: Point prediction for post-tempering hardness (HRC) + ±q90 Conformal Interval.

## Intended Use
- **Primary Use Case**: Research prototype for academic study and pilot candidate assessment.
- **Goal**: To estimate tempering hardness and recommend candidate tempering parameters (T, t) for specific steel alloy families under laboratory conditions.

## Out-of-Scope Use
This model is **NOT** intended for:
- Direct application as a final production recipe in manufacturing facilities.
- Real-time or automated control systems for heat treatment furnaces.
- Automated decision making for out-of-scope or unverified steel families.

## Training Data
- **Shape**: 1466 rows, 17 columns (after data cleaning).
- **Source**: "Tempering data for carbon and low alloy steels" (Kaggle: rgerschtzsauer), CC BY 4.0.
- **File Integrity**: Verified via internal SHA-256 validation. 
- *See [DATA_SHEET.md](DATA_SHEET.md) for full dataset caveats, element ranges, and metadata.*

## Evaluation Data & Methodology
- **Strategy**: 5-Fold GroupKFold Cross-Validation (S2 Split). Grouped by `steel_type` to prevent data leakage and evaluate performance purely on unseen steel grades. 
- *See [EVALUATION.md](EVALUATION.md) for deeper metrics.*

## Quantitative Metrics
- **Mean Absolute Error (MAE)**: 2.35 ± 0.39 HRC (fold-mean) / 2.48 HRC (pooled OOF).
- **R² Score**: 0.94 (fold-mean).
- **Uncertainty Calibration**: Constant-width split-conformal interval `q90 = 5.08 HRC`.
- **Empirical Coverage**: 89.9% globally (see `reports/phase7d_calibration.md` for per-family breakdown).

## Failure Modes & Weaknesses
- **OOD Flatline**: As a tree-based model, XGBoost emits flatline (constant) predictions when inputs exceed the training support.
- **Fixed Interval Boundary**: The ±5.08 HRC conformal band is static. It does NOT automatically widen when evaluating Out-Of-Distribution (OOD) chemistries, potentially resulting in over-confident bounds.
- **Severe Under-Coverage**: 5 specific chemical groups exhibited statistically severe under-coverage compared to the 90% target: AISI-SAE 9264 (Si-spring), AISI-SAE 5160 (Si-spring), AISI-SAE E52100 (bearing), Nitriding Steel, and 0.31%C plain carbon steel.
- **OOF Calibration Nuance**: The empirical coverage is derived from Out-Of-Fold (OOF) residuals inside the training set, meaning practical real-world coverage may be slightly lower due to domain shift or the lack of a fully disjoint external holdout set.

## Limitations
*See [LIMITATIONS.md](LIMITATIONS.md) for the exhaustive list.* Key boundaries include the inability to model specific cooling rates (e.g., oil vs. water quenching) and part geometries, as these were not present in the dataset.

## Human Oversight Requirement
Every prediction and recommendation generated by this prototype necessitates mandatory laboratory verification. A domain expert or metallurgist must review and physically test all recipe candidates (including during commercial pilot phases) prior to any scale-up.
